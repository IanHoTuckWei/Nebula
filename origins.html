The Origins.

Investigating the catalysts of the 2026 atmospheric crisis.

MODULE_00 // IGNOBLE_GASES

A deep-dive analysis into the chemical compounds and volatile particulates compromising air purity.

First let's understand what exactly our air pollution is made out of. In all honesty, it's made out of many things, far too many to list out. However, scientists have discovered 10 of the most prominent among the components of our smog.

GAS_01

Carbon Monoxide (CO)

A toxic, odorless gas produced by the incomplete combustion of fuels in vehicles and industrial machinery. Interferes with oxygen transport in blood.

Extended Diagnostics: Inhaled carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin with an affinity roughly 200 times higher than oxygen, forming carboxyhemoglobin. This prevents effective oxygen delivery throughout the human bloodstream, leading to headaches, tissue damage, and acute respiratory failure during severe atmospheric events.
GAS_02

Nitrogen Oxides (NOx)

Emitted mainly by motor vehicles and power plants. They contribute to acid rain and react with other gases to form harmful smog.

Extended Diagnostics: Consisting primarily of NO and NO2, nitrogen oxides act as key catalysts in the creation of secondary pollutants. When exposed to heat and sunlight, they break down to produce dangerous ground-level ozone, severely aggravating urban respiratory health and damaging surrounding vegetation.
GAS_03

Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)

Released when burning sulfur-containing fuels like coal and oil. Leading cause of acid rain.

Extended Diagnostics: Sulfur dioxide oxidizes in the atmosphere to form sulfurous and sulfuric acid. This leads directly to acid precipitation, causing chemical degradation of soil, acidifying freshwater bodies, and corroding metal and stone architecture across industrial cities.
GAS_04

Ozone (O3)

Ground-level ozone formed when sunlight reacts with pollutants from vehicle and factory emissions. Major component of smog.

Extended Diagnostics: While stratospheric ozone protects Earth from solar radiation, tropospheric (ground-level) ozone acts as an aggressive lung irritant. High concentrations trigger asthma, inflame airways, and drastically reduce agricultural crop yields during peak summer heatwaves.
GAS_05

Ammonia (NH3)

Mostly released from agricultural activities, like livestock farming and fertilizers. Reacts to form dangerous particulate matter.

Extended Diagnostics: Airborne ammonia interacts with nitric and sulfuric acids in the atmosphere to produce ammonium salts. These fine inorganic aerosols account for a substantial fraction of urban particulate pollution, contributing heavily to regional smog cover.
GAS_06

VOCs

Volatile Organic Compounds released from industrial processes, solvents, and fuel combustion. Contribute to smog and global warming.

Extended Diagnostics: VOCs evaporate rapidly at standard room temperature. Beyond their direct toxic and carcinogenic effects on human exposure, VOCs interact with nitrogen oxides under solar radiation, multiplying tropospheric ozone formation and altering regional atmospheric chemistry.
GAS_07

Greenhouse Gases

CO2 and CH4 spikes caused by human activities—such as deforestation, fossil fuel burning, and waste decomposition—driving global warming.

Extended Diagnostics: Massive increases in atmospheric carbon dioxide and methane trap infrared heat within the lower troposphere. This thermal blanket alters global circulation patterns, intensifies seasonal heat anomalies, and creates dry conditions that accelerate peatland combustion.
SCAN_01

PM 2.5

Particulates 30x smaller than hair. These cross the lung-blood barrier upon inhalation.

Extended Diagnostics: Due to their microscopic size (under 2.5 micrometers), these particles bypass nasal filtering mechanisms and lodge deep inside the pulmonary alveoli. From there, toxic heavy metals and chemical residue diffuse directly into systemic blood circulation.
SCAN_02

Benzene

Industrial byproduct trapped by thermal inversions. Highly carcinogenic molecular structure.

Extended Diagnostics: Benzene concentrations spike when stagnant air masses trap urban emissions near the surface. Long-term exposure damages bone marrow, suppresses immune response, and drastically elevates systemic risk for hematologic malignancies like leukemia.
SCAN_03

Peat Smoke

Carbon-heavy discharge from smoldering soil. Releases 10x more CO2 than wood fire.

Extended Diagnostics: Subsurface peat fires burn at lower temperatures with low oxygen, producing incomplete combustion. This generates dense plumes laden with carbon monoxide, complex polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and fine tar droplets that travel hundreds of kilometers across marine corridors.
MODULE_01 // WHAT_CAUSES_THEM

Identifying the primary human activities and industrial mechanisms driving widespread pollution.

To solve atmospheric contamination, we must examine where these compounds originate. These primary drivers stem from a combination of unmonitored industrial growth, destructive land management practices, heavily concentrated urban transport networks, open waste disposal, and heavy construction dust.

CAUSE_01

Industrial Emissions

Heavy factory operations and power generation releasing unfiltered airborne effluents into the troposphere.

Extended Diagnostics: Unchecked manufacturing sectors continuously vent high volumes of sulfur dioxide, heavy metal particulates, and volatile organic compounds into regional air basins, compounding urban smog levels.
CAUSE_02

Peatland Deforestation

Slash-and-burn clearing practices activating deep underground carbon fires across tropical soil beds.

Extended Diagnostics: Draining wetlands and igniting surface brush dries out carbon-dense peat layers below ground. Once ignited, these subsurface fires smolder indefinitely, producing massive particulate plumes.
CAUSE_03

Combustion Transit

Fossil-fuel-powered vehicular gridlock concentrating nitrogen oxides within dense urban corridors.

Extended Diagnostics: High vehicle density combined with urban canyon geography traps tailpipe gases close to street level, accelerating secondary pollutant reactions under daylight heat.
CAUSE_04

Open Waste Incineration

Unregulated burning of municipal plastic and landfill waste releasing persistent toxic chemicals.

Extended Diagnostics: Low-temperature combustion of mixed synthetic waste and plastics emits highly carcinogenic dioxins, furans, and heavy metal particulates directly into residential microclimates.
CAUSE_05

Construction & Fugitive Dust

Uncontained earthwork, demolition, and mineral transport dispersing heavy particulate loads.

Extended Diagnostics: Large-scale infrastructure projects produce high concentrations of coarse airborne silica and mineral particles (PM10 and PM2.5), which degrade local air quality and aggravate respiratory conditions.

MODULE_03 // CHAIN_REACTION Tracking environmental cascading factors from source origin to urban containment. Pollution is rarely localized to its point of origin. Natural wind patterns, chemical reactions, and atmospheric conditions turn isolated emissions into wide-reaching regional hazards through predictable cascading progressions.

01 // The Sumatra-to-Cities Pipeline

Land clearing fires in Indonesia ignite peat soil. Due to the Monsoon Cycle, these winds push particulates across the ocean. Once they hit urban centers, Thermal Inversion traps them between skyscrapers, creating a permanent toxic ceiling.

STEP 01 // IGNITION

Peatland Clearance

Drainage channels lower water tables, turning carbon-dense peatlands into tinderboxes. Uncontrolled surface burns sink deep underground, initiating slow-burning, high-emission fires.
STEP 02 // TRANSPORT

Monsoon Wind Currents

Seasonal wind currents act as a highway for airborne toxins. Mass particulate plumes travel hundreds of miles over open water without dispersing.
STEP 03 // TRAPPING

Thermal Inversion Layer

A warm layer of air settles over the city like a dense lid. Normal vertical air circulation collapses, trapping toxic concentrations directly over streets.

02 // The Solar Ozone Feedback Cascade

Urban traffic emissions release NOx and VOCs during morning rush hours. Intense mid-day solar radiation triggers photochemical reactions that create Ground-Level Ozone, aggravating summer heat domes and respiratory emergency visits.

STEP 01 // PRECURSORS

Morning Rush-Hour Exhaust

Millions of combustion engines release massive volumes of nitrogen dioxide and volatile organic hydrocarbons into urban basins during peak morning commutes.
STEP 02 // PHOTO-OXIDATION

Solar UV Activation

High noon ultraviolet light splits oxygen molecules and binds them with unburned hydrocarbons, accelerating secondary tropospheric ozone creation.
STEP 03 // HEAT DOME

Stagnant Urban Ozone Blanket

Ground-level ozone locks heat near pavement, triggering regional air quality emergency warnings and acute asthma hospitalizations.

03 // Industrial Vapor-to-Acid Rain Cycle

Coal plant stacks release sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides into upper cloud layers. Atmospheric moisture converts these gases into acidic precipitation, destroying downstream forest ecosystems and agricultural soils.

STEP 01 // SMOKESTACK VENTING

Sulfur Oxide Discharge

High-volume coal plants and smelters dump tons of SO2 into high-altitude air streams without sufficient scrubber filtration.
STEP 02 // CONDENSATION

Sulfuric Hydration in Clouds

Airborne water vapor reacts with gaseous oxides, transforming cloud moisture into dilute sulfuric and nitric acid solutions.
STEP 03 // PRECIPITATION

Acid Rainfall & Ecosystem Leaching

Low-pH rainfall strip soil of essential nutrients, corrodes civic infrastructure, and acidifies freshwater aquatic environments.
