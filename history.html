The Haze Timeline

A chronological record tracing severe regional atmospheric crises, policy interventions, and critical environmental shifts.

// 1997
The Great Haze

Widely recognized as the worst environmental disaster in Southeast Asian history, uncontrollable land and peatland fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan blanketed the region in toxic haze for months. Over 70 million people across multiple nations were directly exposed to hazardous PM2.5 levels, resulting in unprecedented public health emergencies, school closures, and economic losses exceeding $9 billion.

// 2002
The ASEAN Agreement

In direct response to recurring transboundary pollution crises, member states convened to establish the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution. Recognized globally as the first regional legally binding treaty of its kind, it created formal framework protocols for joint monitoring, fire prevention systems, emergency responses, and cross-border scientific collaboration.

// 2013
Record-Breaking API

Unusually intense dry weather accelerated agricultural slash-and-burn activities, pushing smog density across the Straits of Malacca to dangerous levels. Singapore’s Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) shattered previous history by reaching an all-time hazardous high of 401, triggering emergency health warnings, widespread mask distribution, and urgent diplomatic negotiations.

// 2015
State of Emergency

Severe El Niño conditions prolonged dry spells, fueling massive peatland fires that released intense carbon emissions. Visibility plunged below 50 meters in Central Kalimantan, causing months of airspace shutdowns, school suspensions affecting millions of children, and emergency military deployments mobilized to suppress subterranean peat fires.

// 2019
The Return of the Smog

Extreme heatwaves and prolonged dry spells provoked widespread fire hotspots across Sumatra and Borneo once again. Massive greenhouse gas releases renewed cross-border diplomatic friction across Southeast Asia, highlighting systemic vulnerabilities in peatland restoration and local environmental enforcement mechanisms.

// 2026
Present Day

Air quality index tracking and atmospheric forecasting have transformed into indispensable components of daily public life. Advanced satellite telemetry, real-time IoT air sensors, and proactive community alert frameworks now define the standard protocol in managing public safety against regional atmospheric degradation.
